# CyberStrike AI sample: a FortiGate and web-shell intrusion at a logistics company

Synthetic network data for **Tallmast Logistics** (`tallmast.example`), a fictional logistics company
with 79 scanned hosts. It replays what public reporting says about **CyberStrikeAI**, the open-source
AI attack platform (LLM agent, 100+ tools, implant builder, WebShell manager) that Team Cymru tied to
the AI-assisted FortiGate campaign Amazon described.

Generated by `vulnflow/sample_cyberstrike.py` (`python3 -c "from vulnflow import sample_cyberstrike as c; c.generate('samples/' + c.FOLDER)"`).
Everything is plain text: no payloads, no binaries, no packet captures.

## What an analyst should find

Window: 2026-11-02 and 11-03 UTC.

1. **fw01 (172.16.10.1).** Two CyberStrikeAI servers (`146.190.195.154`, `64.176.48.93`) fingerprint
   the management ports 443, 8443, 10443 and 4443 at exactly 2-second intervals. `212.11.64.250` then
   runs three 15-minute SSL-VPN login bursts, one attempt every 1.5 s with no jitter. At 04:52 a
   single long VPN session arrives from `185.196.11.225`: valid stolen credentials, no exploit.
   The scan's FortiOS CVE-2024-21762 is P1 because of these alerts, but none of them is an exploit
   for it; the reporting says the campaign used no FortiOS vulnerability.
2. **The VPN address 10.30.250.23** (not scanned; VulnScope lists it as a scanner) sweeps the server
   and user subnets (gogo), runs Nuclei against the DCs, hits Veeam on backup01 with CVE-2023-27532
   (9401) and CVE-2024-40711 (8000), runs DCSync against dc01, and moves to fs01, sql01, erp01 and
   jump01 over SMB and RPC.
3. **backup01 (10.30.0.40)** sends ~1.8 GB to `185.196.11.225` at 07:10.
4. **web01 (172.16.10.20) is P1, "likely exploitation".** Nuclei and sqlmap from `212.11.64.250`;
   the IPS blocks it at 09:14 and the traffic moves to a proxy with a new User-Agent, then to a free
   proxy in the victim's country, then to Tor (`192.0.2.199`). Through Tor, an anonymous upload drops
   `SystemUpdate.aspx`, and the CyberStrikeAI WebShell manager talks to it
   (`Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; CyberStrikeAI-WebShell/1.0)`, an `:OSPROBE_..:END` echo).
   From 10:02 web01 calls `212.11.64.250:8443` every **5.0 seconds with no jitter** until 16:00,
   reaches sql01, dc01 and fs01 for the first time, and the beacon comes back at 06:00 on day 2
   for two hours (the `SystemUpdate` scheduled task).
5. **IOC matches**: all four addresses and both Veeam CVEs on backup01.

## What is real and what is invented

- **Real (published):** `212.11.64.250` and `185.196.11.225` (Amazon; Team Cymru found a CyberStrikeAI
  banner on 8080 at the first), `146.190.195.154` and `64.176.48.93` (Team Cymru's CyberStrikeAI
  server list); the management ports, weak/stolen credentials, gogo, Nuclei, DCSync, pass-the-hash and
  the Veeam CVEs (Amazon); the WebShell User-Agent, `pass` field, OSPROBE echo, `SystemUpdate`
  persistence, 5-second no-jitter check-in and the proxy/User-Agent/Tor fallback (Elastic).
- **Invented:** the company, hosts, internal addresses, dates, which address did what, the C2 port
  (8443), the proxies and Tor exit (documentation addresses), the uploaded file name, signature names
  and sids, and the Nessus plugin IDs for custom findings (`9102xx`). CyberStrikeAI's LLM calls
  (DeepSeek by default) and its Telegram/Slack/DingTalk notifications come from the operator's
  console, so they are not in the victim's traffic.
- Brandefense's article could not be loaded while this sample was built; its overview here is
  written from the other sources.
